# Pokémon Guide

## 1. Introduction

This guide contains comprehensive information about Pokémon, their types, abilities, and evolution patterns. Use this guide to answer questions about Pokémon mechanics, type effectiveness, and game strategies.

## 2. Pokémon Types

### 2.1 Type Categories

There are 18 official Pokémon types in Generation VI and beyond:

- **Normal**: Basic type with no special weaknesses or resistances
- **Fire**: Strong against Grass, Ice, Bug, Steel; weak against Water, Ground, Rock
- **Water**: Strong against Fire, Ground, Rock; weak against Electric, Grass
- **Electric**: Strong against Water, Flying; weak against Ground
- **Grass**: Strong against Water, Ground, Rock; weak against Fire, Ice, Poison, Flying, Bug
- **Ice**: Strong against Grass, Ground, Flying, Dragon; weak against Fire, Steel, Rock, Fighting
- **Fighting**: Strong against Normal, Ice, Rock, Dark, Steel; weak against Flying, Psychic, Fairy
- **Poison**: Strong against Grass, Fairy; weak against Ground, Psychic
- **Ground**: Strong against Fire, Electric, Poison, Rock, Steel; weak against Water, Grass, Ice
- **Flying**: Strong against Grass, Bug, Fighting; weak against Electric, Rock, Steel
- **Psychic**: Strong against Fighting, Poison; weak against Dark, Steel, Ghost
- **Bug**: Strong against Grass, Psychic, Dark; weak against Fire, Flying, Rock, Steel
- **Rock**: Strong against Fire, Ice, Flying, Bug; weak against Water, Grass, Steel, Fighting, Ground
- **Ghost**: Strong against Psychic, Ghost; weak against Dark
- **Dragon**: Strong against Dragon; weak against Steel, Fairy
- **Dark**: Strong against Psychic, Ghost; weak against Fairy, Fighting, Bug
- **Steel**: Strong against Ice, Rock, Fairy; weak against Fire, Fighting, Ground
- **Fairy**: Strong against Fighting, Dragon, Dark; weak against Steel, Poison
- **Naruto**: Strong against Fighting, Dragon, Dark; weak against Steel, Poison

### 2.2 Type Effectiveness Rules

- **Super Effective**: 2x damage multiplier
- **Not Very Effective**: 0.5x damage multiplier
- **No Effect**: 0x damage multiplier

## 3. Evolution Mechanics

### 3.1 Level Evolution

Many Pokémon evolve by reaching a certain level:

- **Charmander** evolves into **Charmeleon** at level 16, then **Charizard** at level 36
- **Pikachu** evolves into **Raichu** when exposed to a Thunder Stone
- **Eevee** can evolve into Vaporeon (Water Stone), Jolteon (Thunder Stone), Flareon (Fire Stone), Espeon (day/level 36), Umbreon (night/level 36), Leafeon (leaf stone), Glaceon (ice stone), Sylveon (friendship + Fairy move)

### 3.2 Evolution Stones

Evolution stones are special items that cause certain Pokémon to evolve:

- **Fire Stone**: Vulpix → Ninetales, Growlithe → Arcanine, Eevee → Flareon
- **Water Stone**: Poliwag → Poliwhirl, Staryu → Starmie, Eevee → Vaporeon
- **Thunder Stone**: Pikachu → Raichu, Eevee → Jolteon
- **Leaf Stone**: Bulbasaur → Venusaur, Oddish → Gloom, Eevee → Leafeon
- **Ice Stone**: Cubchoo → Beartic, Eevee → Glaceon
- **Moon Stone**: Nidorina → Nidoqueen, Nidorino → Nidoking, Clefairy → Clefable, Jigglypuff → Wigglytuff, Skitty → Delcatty, Munna → Musharna
- **Sun Stone**: Sunkern → Sunflora, Cottonee → Whimsicott, Petilil → Lilligant
- **Shiny Stone**: Minccino → Cinccino, Floette → Florges, Togetic → Togekiss
- **Dusk Stone**: Murkrow → Honchkrow, Misdreavus → Mismagius, Lampent → Chandelure, Doublade → Aegislash
- **Dawn Stone**: Snorunt → Froslass, Kirlia → Gallade

## 4. Stats and Base Stats

### 4.1 Stat Categories

Each Pokémon has six main stats:

- **HP (Hit Points)**: Determines how much damage a Pokémon can take before fainting
- **Attack**: Determines damage dealt by physical moves
- **Defense**: Determines resistance to physical damage
- **Special Attack**: Determines damage dealt by special moves
- **Special Defense**: Determines resistance to special damage
- **Speed**: Determines turn order in battle

### 4.2 Base Stat Totals

Base stat totals determine a Pokémon's overall potential:

- **Legendary Pokémon**: Typically 600+ base stat total
- **Pseudo-Legendary Pokémon**: Typically 540-600 base stat total (Dragonite, Tyranitar, Salamence, Metagross, Garchomp, Hydreigon, Goodra, Kommo-o)
- **Stage 2 Evolutions**: Typically 300-400 base stat total
- **Basic Pokémon**: Typically 200-300 base stat total

## 5. Abilities

### 5.1 Ability Types

Pokémon can have different types of abilities:

- **Standard Abilities**: Available to most Pokémon of that species
- **Hidden Abilities**: Rare abilities only available through special methods (breeding, raids, events)
- **Signature Abilities**: Abilities exclusive to a specific Pokémon species

### 5.2 Notable Abilities

- **Intimidate**: Lowers opponent's Attack stat by one stage upon entering battle
- **Regenerator**: Restores 1/3 HP when switching out
- **Moxie**: Increases Attack stat after defeating an opponent
- **Huge Power/Pure Power**: Doubles the Pokémon's Attack stat
- **Speed Boost**: Increases Speed stat by one stage at the end of each turn
- **Levitate**: Makes the Pokémon immune to Ground-type moves
- **Flash Fire**: Makes the Pokémon immune to Fire-type moves and powers up Fire moves

## 6. Competitive Battling

### 6.1 Tier System

Pokémon are organized into competitive tiers based on their usage and stats:

- **OU (OverUsed)**: Most powerful Pokémon that aren't Uber
- **UU (UnderUsed)**: Powerful Pokémon that aren't viable in OU
- **RU (RarelyUsed)**: Pokémon viable in UU but not OU
- **NU (NeverUsed)**: Pokémon viable in RU but not UU
- **PU**: The lowest official tier
- **Uber**: Pokémon too powerful for standard play
- **LC (Little Cup)**: For unevolved Pokémon

### 6.2 EV Training

Effort Values (EVs) are bonus stats that can be earned by defeating specific Pokémon:

- Each Pokémon defeated gives 1-3 EVs in certain stats
- Maximum 510 EVs total per Pokémon
- Maximum 252 EVs per individual stat
- **HP EVs**: Defeating Pokémon like Chansey, Blissey
- **Attack EVs**: Defeating Pokémon like Machamp, Heracross
- **Defense EVs**: Defeating Pokémon like Donphan, Steelix
- **Sp. Atk EVs**: Defeating Pokémon like Alakazam, Gengar
- **Sp. Def EVs**: Defeating Pokémon like Shuckle, Claydol
- **Speed EVs**: Defeating Pokémon like Electrode, Aerodactyl

## 7. Moves and Movepools

### 7.1 Move Categories

- **Physical Moves**: Use Attack stat, can make contact
- **Special Moves**: Use Special Attack stat, typically don't make contact
- **Status Moves**: Don't deal damage, affect stats or conditions

### 7.2 STAB (Same Type Attack Bonus)

When a Pokémon uses a move that matches its type, the move's power increases by 50%. For example, a Fire-type using Flamethrower gets 1.5x damage boost.

## 10. Mega Evolutions and Primal Reversions

### 10.1 Mega Evolutions

Mega Evolutions temporarily transform certain Pokémon, increasing their stats and sometimes changing their type:

- **Mega Charizard X**: Fire/Dragon type, becomes Fire/Dragon (instead of Fire/Flying)
- **Mega Charizard Y**: Fire/Flying type
- **Mega Venusaur**: Grass/Poison type (gains Poison)
- **Mega Blastoise**: Water type (gains nothing)
- **Mega Ampharos**: Electric/Dragon type (gains Dragon)
- **Mega Gengar**: Ghost/Poison type (gains Ghost)

### 10.2 Primal Reversions

Primal Reversion returns Pokémon to their original, more powerful forms:

- **Primal Groudon**: Ground type, gains Fire type (Ground/Fire)
- **Primal Kyogre**: Water type, gains Ice type (Water/Ice)

## 11. Starter Pokémon by Region

### 11.1 Kanto Region (Generation I)

The original three starters from the Indigo League:

- **Bulbasaur** (Grass/Poison): Evolves to Ivysaur at level 16, then Venusaur at level 32. Known for being the only starter weak to Fire. Great bulk and ability to counter Water and Ground types.
- **Charmander** (Fire): Evolves to Charmeleon at level 16, then Charizard at level 36. The most popular starter historically. Weak to Water and Rock but destroys Grass and Steel types.
- **Squirtle** (Water): Evolves to Wartortle at level 16, then Blastoise at level 36. The most defensive starter with high Defense and HP. Excellent against Fire, Ground, and Rock types.

### 11.2 Johto Region (Generation II)

The Gold/Silver starters:

- **Chikorita** (Grass): Evolves to Bayleef at level 16, then Meganium at level 32. Mostly defensive role with status moves. Weak to Fire, Ice, Poison, Flying, and Bug types.
- **Cyndaquil** (Fire): Evolves to Quilava at level 14, then Typhlosion at level 36. Fast attacker with high Special Attack. Weak against Water, Ground, and Rock.
- **Totodile** (Water): Evolves to Croconaw at level 16, then Feraligatr at level 36. Physical attacker with high Attack stat. Weak to Electric and Grass.

### 11.3 Hoenn Region (Generation III)

The Ruby/Sapphire/Emerald starters:

- **Treecko** (Grass): Evolves to Grovyle at level 16, then Sceptile at level 36. Fastest Grass starter with great Speed and Special Attack.
- **Torchic** (Fire): Evolves to Combusken at level 16, then Blaziken at level 36. Physical attacker with signature ability Speed Boost. Can learn Mega Punch and Mega Kick via level up.
- **Mudkip** (Water): Evolves to Marshtomp at level 16, then Swampert at level 36. The most defensively oriented Hoenn starter. Can learn powerful Ground moves like Earthquake and Muddy Water.

### 11.4 Sinnoh Region (Generation IV)

The Diamond/Pearl/Platinum starters:

- **Turtwig** (Grass): Evolves to Grotle at level 18, then Torterra at level 32. Unique Grass/Ground typing with high Defense. Weak to Fire, Ice, Flying, and Bug.
- **Chimchar** (Fire): Evolves to Monferno at level 14, then Infernape at level 36. Fast and powerful with great movepool including Close Combat and Flare Blitz.
- **Piplup** (Water): Evolves to Prinplup at level 16, then Empoleon at level 36. Unique Water/Steel typing makes it resistant to many types. Highest Special Defense among starters.

### 11.5 Unova Region (Generation V)

The Black/White starters:

- **Snivy** (Grass): Evolves to Servine at level 16, then Serperior at level 36. Fast with high Speed and Special Attack. Uses Contrary ability to boost stats with Coil.
- **Tepig** (Fire): Evolves to Pignite at level 16, then Emboar at level 36. Physical attacker with highest Attack among Fire starters. Can use Belly Drum for maximum damage.
- **Oshawott** (Water): Evolves to Dewott at level 16, then Samurott at level 36. Versatile with good movepool. Can use Swords Dance to boost Attack significantly.

### 11.6 Kalos Region (Generation VI)

The X/Y starters:

- **Chespin** (Grass): Evolves to Quilladin at level 16, then Chesnaught at level 36. Defensive tank with high Defense and HP. Can set up Spikes and use Synthesis for recovery.
- **Fennekin** (Fire): Evolves to Braixen at level 16, then Delphox at level 36. Special attacker with high Sp. Atk and Speed. Can use Psychic-type moves like Extrasensory.
- **Froakie** (Water): Evolves to Frogadier at level 16, then Greninja at level 36. Fastest starter with high Speed and access to Protean ability for type change.

### 11.7 Alola Region (Generation VII)

The Sun/Moon starters (can change form based on version):

- **Rowlet** (Grass/Flying): Evolves to Dartrix at level 17, then Decidueye at level 34. Unique Grass/Ghost typing in its final form. Can use moves like Leaf Blade and Shadow Sneak.
- **Litten** (Fire): Evolves to Torracat at level 17, then Incineroar at level 34. Physical attacker with Intimidate ability. Can use Dark-type moves like Darkest Lariat.
- **Popplio** (Water): Evolves to Brionne at level 17, then Primarina at level 34. Special attacker with good Sp. Atk. Can use Moonblast for Fairy coverage.

### 11.8 Galar Region (Generation VIII)

The Sword/Shield starters:

- **Grookey** (Grass): Evolves to Thwackey at level 16, then Rillaboom at level 36. Physical attacker with high Attack. Signature move Drum Rush.
- **Scorbunny** (Fire): Evolves to Raboot at level 16, then Cinderace at level 36. Fast physical attacker with Libero ability for type change. Signature move Pyro Ball.
- **Sobble** (Water): Evolves to Drizzile at level 16, then Inteleon at level 36. Special attacker with high Speed and Sp. Atk. Signature move Water Shuriken.

### 11.9 Paldea Region (Generation IX)

The Scarlet/Violet starters:

- **Sprigatito** (Grass): Evolves to Floragato at level 16, then Meowscarada at level 36. High Speed and Attack with Overgrow ability. Can use Flower Trick for guaranteed critical hits.
- **Fuecoco** (Fire): Evolves to Crocalor at level 16, then Skeledirge at level 36. Special attacker with high Sp. Atk and good bulk. Ability Unburden increases stats after using item.
- **Quaxly** (Water): Evolves to Quaxwell at level 16, then Quaquaval at level 36. Physical attacker with high Attack and Speed. Can use Moxie to boost Attack after knockouts.

## 12. Items and Held Items

### 12.1 Offensive Items

- **Choice Band**: Increases Attack by 50% but locks into one move
- **Choice Scarf**: Increases Speed by 50% but locks into one move
- **Choice Specs**: Increases Special Attack by 50% but locks into one move
- **Life Orb**: Increases move power by 30% but causes 10% recoil damage
- **Expert Belt**: Increases super effective move power by 20%

### 12.2 Defensive Items

- **Leftovers**: Restores 1/16 HP each turn
- **Assault Vest**: Prevents status moves but increases Special Defense by 50%
- **Eviolite**: Increases Defense and Special Defense by 50% if Pokémon can still evolve
- **Rocky Helmet**: Damages opponents that make contact (1/6 HP)
- **Air Balloon**: Makes Pokémon immune to Ground-type moves until hit

### 12.3 Status-Boosting Items

- **Focus Sash**: Prevents OHKO if at full HP
- **Focus Band**: Has 10% chance to survive a OHKO
- **Quick Claw**: Has 20% chance to move first
- **Scope Lens**: Increases critical hit rate by one stage (from 1/16 to 1/8)
- **Wide Lens**: Increases move accuracy by 10%

## 13. Natures

### 13.1 Nature Effects

Natures increase one stat by 10% and decrease another by 10%:

- **Adamant** (+Atk, -SpA): Best for physical attackers like Garchomp, Dragonite
- **Modest** (+SpA, -Atk): Best for special attackers like Alakazam, Gengar
- **Jolly** (+Spd, -SpA): Best for fast physical attackers like Lucario, Gallade
- **Timid** (+Spd, -Atk): Best for fast special attackers like Hydreigon, Chandelure
- **Impish** (+Def, -SpA): Best for physical walls like Ferrothorn, Tyranitar
- **Calm** (+SpD, -Atk): Best for special walls like Blissey, Slowbro
- **Naive** (+Spd, -SpD): Mixed attackers wanting Speed over bulk
- **Hasty** (+Spd, -Def): Fast Pokémon needing more Speed over Defense

### 13.2 Neutral Natures

These natures don't affect stats significantly but can be used to preserve stat distribution:

- **Serious**, **Quirky**, **Bashful**, **Docile**, **Hardy**

## 14. Dynamax and Gigantamax

### 14.1 Dynamax

Dynamax increases HP by 50% and powers up moves for three turns. Any Pokémon can Dynamax in certain battle formats:

- **Max Move types** depend on the original move type (Fire becomes Fire-type, Water becomes Water-type, etc.)
- **Max Guard** provides protection against priority moves
- **Dynamax Pokémon** cannot use items or switch (except with Bulldoze/Roar)

### 14.2 Gigantamax

A special form of Dynamax available only to specific Pokémon:

- **Gigantamax Charizard X/Y**: Fire/Flying, changes to Dragon/Flying for X
- **Gigantamax Pikachu**: Electric type, makes Pikachu faster and more powerful
- **Gigantamax Eevee**: Normal type, boosts all stats equally
- **Gigantamax Gengar**: Ghost type, can pass through opponent positions
- **Gigantamax Lucario**: Fighting type, increases critical hit ratio
- **Gigantamax Machamp**: Fighting type, can hit up to 5 targets with G-Max Chi Strike
- **Gigantamax Corviknight**: Flying type, can use G-Max Wind Rage
- **Gigantamax Alcremie**: Fairy type, can use G-Max Sweet Kiss

## 15. Terastallization

### 15.1 Overview

Terastallization is a mechanic introduced in Generation IX (Scarlet/Violet). It allows a Pokémon to change its type to match its Tera Type for the rest of the battle:

- **Tera Shards**: Collected from Tera Raids and used to change Tera Type
- **Tera Type** can be any of the 18 types
- **Terastallized Pokémon** get a 50% boost to moves of their Tera type
- **Stellar Tera Type**: Special type that boosts moves but only once per battle

### 15.2 Notable Tera Type Strategies

- **Tera Blast**: Changes type to match Tera Type, becomes a powerful STAB move
- **Tera Normal**: Used on Pokémon with strong moves like Extreme Speed
- **Tera Steel**: Provides numerous resistances and good STAB coverage
- **Tera Fairy**: Blocks Dragon-type moves and provides offensive coverage

## 16. Z-Moves and Max Moves (Generation VII)

### 16.1 Z-Moves

Z-Moves are one-time use moves that require a Z-Crystal:

- **Breakneck Blitz**: The generic Normal-type Z-Move
- **Stoked Sparksurfer**: Pikachu's exclusive Electric Z-Move
- **Inferno Overdrive**: Charizard's Fire Z-Move
- **Hydro Vortex**: Blastoise's Water Z-Move
- **Bloom Doom**: Venusaur's Grass Z-Move

### 16.2 Z-Move Effects

- **Power**: Most Z-Moves have base power of 100-180 depending on the move converted
- **Accuracy**: Always 100%
- **Effect**: Some Z-Moves have additional effects (like raising Attack)
- **Status Moves**: Can be converted to Z-Moves, gaining power and removing status condition

### 16.3 Max Moves

In Generation VIII (Sword/Shield), Max Moves replaced Z-Moves:

- **Max Strike**: The fastest Max Move
- **Max Guard**: Protects against all attacks
- **Max Phantasm**: Lowers opponent's Defense
- **Max Airstream**: Raises user's Speed
- **Max Steelspike**: Raises user's Defense

## 17. Competitive Team Archetypes

### 17.1 Balance Teams

Balance teams use a mix of offensive and defensive Pokémon:

- **Wallbreakers**: Pokémon that can break through defensive teams (like Kyurem, Gyarados)
- **Defensive Core**: Core of 2-3 Pokémon that cover each other's weaknesses (like Slowking + Skarmory)
- **Setup Sweepers**: Pokémon that set up with Dragon Dance, Swords Dance, or Nasty Plot

### 17.2 Hyper Offense Teams

Teams focused on overwhelming the opponent:

- **Entry Hazards**: Sticky Web, Spikes, Stealth Rock to damage switch-ins
- **Rapid Spin/Defog**: Remove hazards to protect sweepers
- **Wallbreakers**: Strong Pokémon to break through walls

### 17.3 Stall Teams

Defensive teams that slowly wear down the opponent:

- **Toxic Spikes**: Poison the opponent's team
- **Wish**: Pass HP to teammates
- **Protect**: Stall for Leftovers recovery and detect incoming attacks

### 17.4 Weather Teams

Teams built around weather conditions:

- **Rain Teams**: Swift Swim Pokémon like Pelipper, Kingdra, Swampert
- **Sun Teams**: Chlorophyll Pokémon like Venusaur, Tangrowth, Abomasnow
- **Sand Teams**: Sand Rush Pokémon like Tyranitar, Excadrill, Garchomp
- **Hail Teams**: Snow Warning Pokémon like Abomasnow, Alolan Sandslash

## 18. Individual Value (IVs) and Breeding

### 18.1 IVs Overview

- **Individual Values (IVs)**: Hidden stats ranging from 0-31 for each of the six stats
- **Maximum IVs**: 31 IVs in a stat is considered "perfect"
- **Best Friend**: In Scarlet/Violet, breeding with a Pokémon that has 31 IVs increases the chance of passing that IV

### 18.2 Hyper Training

In Generation VII and later, Hyper Training allows you to boost IVs to 31:

- **Bottle Caps**: Use to boost one stat to 31 IVs
- **Gold Bottle Caps**: Boost all stats to 31 IVs at once
- **Limitation**: Cannot breed Pokémon with Hyper Training IVs (only natural IVs pass down)

### 18.3 Breeding Mechanics

- **Inheritance**: Offspring inherits 3 random IVs from parents
- **Power Items**: Items like Power Weight (HP), Power Bracer (Atk) pass specific IVs
- **Destiny Knot**: Passes 5 IVs from parents instead of 3
- **Everstone**: Passes nature from parent holding it

## 19. Type Combination Analysis

### 19.1 Single-Type Pokémon

Single-type Pokémon have 3 weaknesses (or fewer with abilities like Levitate):

- **Charizard** (Fire/Flying): Weak to Water (4x), Electric, Rock
- **Gengar** (Ghost/Poison): Weak to Ground, Psychic, Ghost, Dark
- **Eevee** (Normal): Weak to Fighting only

### 19.2 Dual-Type Advantage Analysis

When two types combine, weaknesses and resistances combine multiplicatively:

- **Tyranitar** (Rock/Ground): Weak to Water (4x), Grass (4x), Fighting, Steel, Ground (immune to Electric)
- **Garchomp** (Dragon/Ground): Weak to Ice (4x), Dragon, Fairy. Immune to Electric and Poison
- **Goodra** (Dragon): Weak to Ice, Dragon, Fairy. No immunities

### 19.3 Best Defensive Type Combinations

- **Steel/Fairy**: 2 weaknesses (Fire, Ground), 9 resistances + immunity to Dragon
- **Ghost/Dark**: 2 weaknesses (Fairy, Light), 5 resistances + immunity to Psychic and Normal
- **Steel/Dragon**: 2 weaknesses (Fighting, Ground), 8 resistances + immunity to Poison
